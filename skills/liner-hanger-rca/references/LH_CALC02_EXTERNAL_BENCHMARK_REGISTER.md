## Candidate G — independently published 3D high-tension force/torque analytical limit (2026-11-15)

**Primary, openly accessible peer-reviewed article:** M. Fazaelizadeh, G. Hareland and B. S. Aadnoy (2010), *Application of New 3-D Analytical Model for Directional Wellbore Friction*, *Modern Applied Science* 4(2), DOI **10.5539/mas.v4n2p2**, equations **(5), (6) and (8)**; equivalent combined-motion forms **(13) and (14)**. Publisher: https://www.ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/mas/article/view/4418/4229 ; open PDF: https://www.rocsoltech.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Application-of-New-3-D-Analytical-Model-for-Directional-Wellbore-Friction-MAS-Journal.pdf . The publisher identifies the paper as CC BY 4.0. Source equations, citations and constants are attributed; no full article was copied into the skill.

**Exact model boundary of comparison:** the publication treats curvature-friction normal force as tension-dominated/weightless *inside the bend* and adds buoyed gravity after the bend. Our versioned minimum-curvature vector model instead retains weight in its contact normal along the bend. They are **different physical approximations at finite nonzero weight** and should *not* be expected to agree exactly for realistic low-tension wells, especially where pipe gravity controls wall contact, wall-side switching or finite stiffness matters. The published 3D contact curvature for endpoint tangents is dogleg `beta=acos(sin(i0)sin(i1)cos(azi1-azi0)+cos(i0)cos(i1))`. With bottom tension `F1`, axial coefficient `mu`, buoyed segment weight `W`, inclination radians `i0,i1`, source's planar static-weight approximation `W(sin(i1)-sin(i0))/(i1-i0)`, and `beta`, the paper's limiting pickup/slackoff top force is `F1 exp(± mu beta)+W(sin(i1)-sin(i0))/(i1-i0)`; pure rotation (`psi=0`) has `F1+static_weight`, and paper's leading torque is `mu_rot r F1 beta`. These are equations reproduced **independently of any production solver** in a test file (no imported production force equation). This example's `mu_rot` differs from the article's common friction symbol only as the skill's *explicit independent rotary coefficient* choice, not a published separate coefficient.

**Executed reproducible comparison:** `tests/test_published_3d_high_tension_limit.py` commit `aa9c3cd` uses existing explicitly *fictional* `examples/synthetic_torque_drag_3d.json` with a combined 30°→60° inclination and 0°→60° azimuth turn over 100m, nonzero buoyed weight **~11,937.21 N**, source-independent fictional bottom tensile boundary **2,000,000 N** (mathematical asymptotic test only, **NOT a credible operating force/setpoint**), `mu_ax=0.2, mu_rot=0.3, r=0.05m`. It checks four scalar observables against separately calculated paper high-tension equations at **<1% relative difference**, verifies the pickup and slackoff **relative** asymptotic discrepancy decreases when the imaginary boundary increases from 200kN to 2000kN, and checks `unreviewed_inputs`/field exclusion is retained. **7 tests passed within the complete regression**: GitHub Actions [run 43736419628](https://github.com/smbabalola/liner-hanger-project/actions/runs/43736419628), Python **3.11 629 passed (24.23s)** and Python **3.12 629 passed (26.99s)**, zero failures/skips, authenticated by both job logs. No separate per-test local execution is claimed. The 1% tolerance is a **limited asymptotic theory comparison**, not a field prediction tolerance.

**Gate outcome: restricted independent published analytical-force LIMIT CHECK PASSED, full LH-CALC-02 physical gate remains OPEN.** The journal's article also plots field hookload predictions but does not provide a complete matched 3D survey, exact as-run tubular/string source inputs, independently tabulated force vectors, full uncertainty/pressure/cable-friction corrections or blind friction calibration for an apples-to-apples full-well validation in the inspected source. This limited source check must not be represented as an independently reproduced **full nonzero-weight 3D well** or a qualified reviewer/OEM sign-off. No change to the force solver was required.
